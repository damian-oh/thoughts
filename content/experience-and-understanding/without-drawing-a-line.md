When I was in a band in Canada, we were rehearsing a song, and the vocalist, who was also the bandleader, asked me to follow the vocal melody on lead guitar during the pre-chorus and chorus.

I had a different idea. I told him I would try to find another approach.

I experimented with several options, but none of them worked well. In the end, I decided to follow the vocal melody in the pre-chorus and play open chords with a capo in the chorus. Even then, I tried to make the melody part sound distinctly like a guitar. I played the notes across the first three strings to make them ring out together, and I handled the larger leaps with slides and bends. Once the arrangement was finished, I was satisfied with it.

If the vocalist had not asked me to follow the melody, I would not have arrived at that result.

Working with AI has given me a different kind of experience.

With AI, I write, code, and make music far faster than I could on my own. I do not feel that quality is sacrificed for speed. Often, I think the results are better.

It is true that I spend less time working through every detail or solving every problem by myself. After publishing around forty essays on this website, however, the questions I want to explore have become much clearer. I do not know when, how, or even whether I would have reached that point without AI.

AI does two things for me. It helps bring what is already in my mind into the world quickly, and it sometimes shows me possibilities I would not have considered.

Not long ago, a composer friend in South Korea told me about his experience using [Suno](https://suno.com/discover).

"When I compose and arrange, I can never completely escape my own taste," he said. "Suno sometimes shows me a result not affected by my own preferences and tendencies. It feels like a great partner. The quality is good, too. There is no reason not to use it."

I feel the same way. I see no reason not to work with AI.

I try not to draw a strict line between working with people and working with AI. If a situation requires a person, or if I want to work with one, I can work with a person. If a task calls for AI, or if I want to collaborate with AI, I can do that instead.

As long as no one is harmed and the result is good, the method itself matters less to me.
