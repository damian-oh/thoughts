Recently, I read a short note I had written in Korean in 2019, during my first year at Howon University. It was a response to Romain Gary's *The Birds Come to Die in Peru*.

In the note, I had written down a conversation from a Tuesday evening on the bus back to Seoul. I was sitting next to a classmate who played drums, and we were talking about what makes something art.

He brought up a story, perhaps the well-known prank from a few years earlier, about someone leaving a pair of glasses on the floor of an art gallery. Within minutes, visitors had gathered around the glasses, as though they were part of the exhibition.

My classmate's point, as far as I remember, was about the limits of subjective interpretation. If the glasses had been dropped by accident, could the audience's attention turn them into art?

Reading my old note, I noticed how quickly I had searched for a counterexample.

If the glasses could not be art because they had ended up there by accident rather than human intention, I wondered how we should explain the Grand Canyon. People stand in the middle of natural landscapes and experience something not so different from what they might feel in a gallery. Sometimes they even call it art. If intention is the boundary, the canyon falls outside it. If the viewer's experience is the boundary, the glasses fall inside it.

At the time, I thought I was trying to resolve or explore a question about aesthetics. Looking at the note seven years later, I am more interested in the way I handled the problem.

Even then, my method was to test a claim by trying to break it. I did not look for a positive definition of art. Instead, I looked for a case that might challenge the proposed boundary. I might have wanted a clean, explicit line that would clarify the point where one thing ended and the other began.

I still do not have a rule that separates the glasses from the canyon. I may keep looking for one. But I have begun to recognize how easily the search for a definitive answer can become [[Rigour as Paralysis|something that consumes me]].

Whether or not I found an answer, the bus was stilling carrying me back to Seoul.
