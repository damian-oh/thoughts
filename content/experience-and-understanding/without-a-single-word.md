In my third year of middle school, we had an opportunity to review how the written-response questions on a mathematics exam had been marked.

I happened to see a classmate's answer.

Until then, I had always written my mathematical solutions using a considerable amount of natural language. I would connect equations with phrases such as "therefore" or "which means that", explaining how one step led to another.

My classmate's answer was different. There was not a single word in it. The entire solution consisted of numbers, letters, and mathematical symbols. Yet I could follow the same reasoning that I had expressed in my own answer. My classmate had managed to present the entire sequence of steps without using any of the sentences I had relied on.

Neither of us had lost any marks.

I remember finding the difference remarkable. My classmate's solution was more concise than mine, and somehow seemed a little more mathematical.

I am not sure exactly why I felt that way. Both answers were correct. The reasoning was there in either case. My classmate had simply expressed it in a form I had never thought to use.

I could not tell whether we had actually thought about the problem differently. The absence of natural language in a written solution did not mean that my classmate had reasoned without it. Nor did my use of words establish that I had needed them to think through the problem. What I could see was the difference between the answers.

I continued writing my solutions much as I always had. I still used natural language, even after seeing that it was possible to express the same reasoning without it.

But I did not forget the answer I had seen.

Sometimes, when studying on my own, I would try to remove the words from my solutions. I would look at the equations and consider whether the relationships between them were clear without the explanations I had written.

I was not trying to correct a mistake. My original approach had worked, and I had no reason to abandon it. I simply wanted to see whether I could express the same reasoning another way.

I continued using words in my exams.

But from time to time, I would sit with a solution and try to write it without a single one.
