A few days ago, I met someone at the Rankin Innovation Hub in Niagara Falls. During our conversation, he mentioned that he had met Elon Musk during his university years.

He described Musk as an excellent marketer and businessman, but not necessarily an exceptional engineer. He believed there was a distinction between someone who understands engineering well enough to lead a team of engineers and someone who does the engineering himself. He also expressed his frustration with the way Musk sometimes appears to receive personal credit for work carried out by the engineers at SpaceX.

I found the conversation interesting.

Most of what I know about Musk comes from what I have seen or read in the media. I do not think I know enough to make a well-founded judgment about him and his engineering abilities.

But I was not particularly concerned with deciding whether he was right.

What stayed with me was something different. Had I not been there, at that time, in that place, I would not have heard his story.

Had I visited on another day, left a little earlier, or simply not started a conversation with him, I might never have learned what he thought of Musk. Perhaps I could have found a similar opinion somewhere on the internet. But I would not have heard it from that particular person, in that particular conversation.

I began to wonder how many other stories I had never heard.

There must be countless people whose paths I have never crossed, each with experiences and perspectives I know nothing about. Some might have told me something fascinating. Others might have said nothing I would remember. Most of them will probably remain strangers to me.

I cannot even know what I have missed.

There is something subtle about that. I can reconsider what I have heard, question what I believe, and search for information I know I lack. But what about the things I have never encountered, the questions I have never thought to ask, or the possibilities I have never had reason to consider?

I do not know how much of what I understand about the world depends on where I have happened to be, whom I have happened to meet, and what I have happened to hear.

Some encounters may change my life. Others may be so ordinary that they are forgotten by the following morning. Some may feel precious, while others may be painful. A precious encounter does not necessarily have to teach me anything, just as a painful one does not necessarily have to become a lasting burden.

I am not sure that every experience offers something beyond the experience itself.

This does not mean that my choices make no difference. I can decide to approach someone, continue a conversation, pursue a relationship, or walk away. I can act on what I know and try to change what happens next. Sometimes my actions may bring about exactly what I hoped for.

But things do not always turn out as I expect.

Even if I could know in advance that a particular encounter would change my life, I am not sure I could know what that change would look like, whether it would happen as anticipated, or how I would eventually understand it. Knowing something about a possible outcome might influence my choices, but those choices would not give me complete control over what followed.

I suppose the same is true of many things beyond encounters.

I will live, and eventually I will die. In the meantime, I will meet people and part from them. Some things will begin, and others will end. I will learn certain things and lose others. I may try to hold on to something, or decide to let it go.

Some of these things will matter greatly to me. Others may not.

I do not know that I need to settle what they all mean.

A meeting is a meeting. A parting is a parting. A beginning is a beginning, and an ending is an ending.

None of this prevents an encounter from becoming meaningful. Nor does it guarantee that it will. I may come to treasure something I once thought insignificant, or eventually forget something that once seemed important.

Perhaps I will understand some of these experiences differently as time passes. Perhaps I will never think about them again.

That day at the Innovation Hub, I happened to meet someone who had a story to tell. I listened, asked a few questions, and heard his perspective on a person I knew mostly through the media.

I do not know whether that conversation will mean anything more to me in the future. But I enjoyed hearing his story.

Had I not been there, at that time, in that place, I would not have heard it.
