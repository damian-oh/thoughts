In my second year of middle school, I met a friend who wanted to become a playwright.

At the beginning of the school year, everyone in our class took turns introducing themselves. I said that I wanted to become a musician. Later that day, he said that he liked music, too.

We gradually became close friends.

He was smart, did well at school, and was a good writer. He also enjoyed sports, and we played football and other sports together once or twice a week. He led the school's drama club, writing his own plays and putting them on stage. I joined the club to take care of the music.

Until then, I had made music I wanted to make. Working on his plays was different. For the first time, I had to think about what kind of music would suit a particular scene or line of dialogue.

I also began listening to and playing musical theatre songs for the first time. I did not know much music theory at the time, but I could tell that some of the chord progressions were different from those I was used to hearing in pop and rock music.

Another thing I enjoyed was reading his scripts. Whenever my friend finished writing one, he would show it to me.

I remember being impressed almost every time I read one. I would think that if the play could be performed as he had written it, it would make an excellent production.

Then, one day, he showed me a script that I did not like. I told him something like:

"Every time I've read one of your scripts, I've thought it would be great if we could put it on stage just as you wrote it. But this time, I don't feel that way. I don't think this one is as good as the others."

He began revising the script, but the play was never staged.

A few months later, he told me something I still remember. He said that my assessment had shaken him considerably. He had tried hard to rewrite the script, but he had not been able to finish it.

I no longer remember what the script was about. I cannot say whether my assessment was reasonable. Nor do I remember what I thought when he told me how much my words had affected him.

I still think that I should have told him what I thought, although I could have [[The Way I Would Rather Speak|expressed it differently]].

He had shown me his scripts before, and I had told him when I liked them. I took his decision to show them to me as an indication that he wanted to know what I thought. When I did not like one as much, I told him that, too.

He was just as direct with me. Some time after we graduated from middle school, he told me that he did not think I had a mind for math or science, and that if I had, I would have been better at writing songs.

I did not know whether he was right about the kind of mind I had. But I largely agreed with his assessment of my songwriting.

He later studied theatre and worked in professional theatre productions. Years later, after completing my first year of studies in Canada, I returned to South Korea for a break and met him again.

He told me that he was planning to go to Australia to study cooking.

He also told me that working in theatre had left him with some skepticism about the field, although I did not know the details. I do not know exactly what led him to choose cooking, either.

We have continued to keep in touch.

Recently, I showed him this website.

He asked me to write about him, too.
