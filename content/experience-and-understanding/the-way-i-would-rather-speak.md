I have noticed that I tend to be a little kinder when speaking English and a little more cynical when speaking Korean.

The difference is not that dramatic. It is mostly noticeable in ordinary conversations, and I do not think my inner judgments change very much when I switch languages.

What changes is how I express them.

I might respond to the same idea with a slightly more cynical tone in Korean, while choosing a gentler expression in English. I can disagree with someone in either language, but the way I communicate that disagreement seems to differ slightly.

I am not sure why.

Perhaps it has something to do with the languages themselves. Perhaps it comes from the different environments in which I have learned to use them, or the habits I have developed through the people I have spoken with.

I do not know how much of the difference can be attributed to language, culture, familiarity, or something else entirely. But there is another difference I have noticed, one that has less to do with which language I am speaking.

I used to prefer the cynical way of expressing myself.

I am also not sure why I preferred it. Looking back, I suspect that I associated a certain distance from the world with the ability to examine it more objectively.

I wanted to be able to make judgments independently of my circumstances, my preferences, and the expectations of those around me. I did not want to accept something merely because it was comforting, socially convenient, or widely believed.

Perhaps cynicism seemed like one way of maintaining that independence.

If I could stand apart from something, I might be able to see it more clearly.

I do not know whether I actually thought about it in those terms. This is an interpretation I am making now, and I may be making my earlier attitudes seem more coherent than they really were.

I no longer find myself preferring that way of speaking.

I have become considerably kinder, even when speaking Korean. The difference between the two languages remains, but it is smaller than it used to be.

More importantly, I now prefer the kinder way of expressing myself.

I do not think this means that I have become less interested in objectivity. I still want to examine claims carefully, distinguish what I know from what I merely suspect, and avoid accepting conclusions without sufficient evidence.

Nor do I think that kindness makes a judgment more accurate.

A carefully expressed disagreement can still be mistaken. A cynical remark can still identify a genuine problem. The tone of a statement does not determine whether the judgment it expresses is justified.

But I have become less convinced that maintaining an independent judgment requires maintaining a distance from the person with whom I am speaking.

I can disagree with someone without feeling obliged to take their argument apart.

I can be more affirming when someone's thoughts resonate with my own.

Perhaps what has changed is not my interest in objectivity, but my interest in participation.

I seem to have become more interested in interacting with the people and things I encounter in this life, rather than primarily examining them from a distance. I do not think these two attitudes are mutually exclusive. Participating in something does not necessarily prevent me from evaluating it, just as keeping my distance does not guarantee that my evaluation will be accurate.

But there may be a difference between wanting to understand the world and wanting to understand it while remaining apart from it. I suspect I used to place more importance on the latter than I do now.

I also do not want to suggest that kindness is always preferable. There may be situations in which a direct, uncomfortable response is necessary. Speaking gently can sometimes become a way of avoiding what needs to be said.

I would not want to exchange honesty for the appearance of kindness. However, I am no longer sure that I need to choose between them.

There are things I still disagree with, arguments I still find unconvincing, and conclusions I am unwilling to accept. I do not expect that to change simply because I would rather speak differently.

What has changed, at least to some extent, is how I want to carry those judgments into my interactions with others.

I still notice that I tend to sound a little kinder in English. But increasingly, I find that I want to speak that way in Korean, too.

To some extent, I already do.
