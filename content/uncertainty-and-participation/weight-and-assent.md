In school, I played center-back. Behind you there is one man, and then the net. Most people treat the last line as a burden, a place where every mistake is fatal. I loved it for the opposite reason.

I was a defender who relied on overwhelming speed and physical dominance, with an exclusive right and responsibility to control the team's offside line. I often told my fellow defenders, "Push up past me," and then raised the line higher, waiting for the attackers to step into my territory. A single man behind me would have played the whole attack onside. I would rather trust my own recovery than let anyone stand there.

There was a distinct satisfaction in facing an attack directly, shutting it down entirely, and moving into the next play as if nothing had occurred. On the last line the game was not an open-ended debate. You stopped the ball or you did not. The outcome was immediate and verifiable, and it belonged to me.

I find the same thing now in powerlifting. I unrack 530 lb (240 kg), brace, and take the first inch of the descent. My attention narrows to one fact: if I lose focus here, I get hurt.

There is no room for contemplation under that. The questions that usually run underneath everything do not get answered: Do I actually want this, or do I only think I do? Is this decision justified, or merely arbitrary? Am I meaning this, or projecting meaning onto it? They stop being asked. The barbell does not care about nuance, provisionality, or interpretation. It is a physical fact, and it demands execution.

What I get from it is not relief from difficulty. It is assent. There is no gap between the act and me, no part of the lift I perform while withholding consent to performing it. I am entirely inside it. The reason is not that I have resolved anything. It is that the platform has removed the conditions under which resolution is required. Nothing there is open to interpretation, so nothing is left to suspend. It is a closed system I can stand in with my whole body: a secondary dominant made of iron.

When the ambiguity gets too heavy, I go and pick up something heavier that is not ambiguous, and afterward the abstractions waiting on the other side feel lighter. Sometimes they seem to vanish altogether. That is the part to watch. A reset that works that well is difficult to distinguish from [[Rigour as Paralysis|a refined permission not to participate]], and the difference is not visible from the inside. The lift is honest about what it is. I am the one who has to be honest about what I am using it for.

I go back under the bar anyway. Firmly, and on the understanding that the platform is somewhere I start from.
