I began supporting the Doosan Bears, a Korean baseball team based in Seoul, when I was eight or nine.

It started because of my [[What I Hope to Be Able to Say#^father|father]]. He had supported the Bears since he was a child, when the KBO was first established.

I remember sitting with him in front of the television in 2015, watching the Bears win their first championship in fourteen years.

That season marked the beginning of an era.

Throughout my school years in the 2010s, the Bears were almost always competing for the championship. Supporting a team over a long period means learning to measure time through rosters. I still remember an earlier generation of players: Lee Jong-wook, Ko Young-min, Son Si-heon, and Kim Dong-joo, gradually giving way to the core that defined the team's prime: Oh Jae-won, Kim Jae-ho, Yang Eui-ji, and Dustin Nippert.

I loved their competitive spirit and their refusal to give a game away.

Even during those years, however, the moments that stayed with me most vividly were not only the championships.

One occurred in August 2016, involving the relief pitcher Chung Jae-hoon. He had spent most of his career pitching in high-leverage situations. However, when the Bears won the title in 2015, he was not there. He had left the team for a single season as compensation when Doosan signed a free agent, before returning the following year.

In 2016, back in a Doosan uniform, he was once again carrying the bullpen. Every fan I knew shared the same hope that he would be the one to throw the final pitch and seal the championship.

Then, late in the season, a hard-hit ball struck his right forearm on the mound. Even with his pitching arm fractured, he picked up the ball with his left hand and tried to throw to first base to record the out. It was his last appearance of the year. The Bears went on to sweep the Korean Series in four games, winning the championship without him on the field.

Another moment came in late August 2019. At the time, the Bears were in second place, chasing first-place SK by a wide gap. On August 28, in a game against SK, Oh Jae-won was on third base when he suddenly stole home plate. I remember that play as one of the defining moments of the team's remarkable late-season run. The Bears went on to close the gap and eventually claimed first place on the final day of the regular season.

Those two plays still represent what the team felt like to me back then. A veteran trying to throw the ball with his non-pitching hand after breaking his arm, and a runner breaking for home when no one expected him to try.

After 2019, I stopped watching baseball.

I do not quite know why. My life moved on, I eventually left Korea, and the Bears went through more difficult seasons in the 2020s. For several years, I rarely thought about them.

On October 12, 2026, the Bears played their final regular-season game at Jamsil Baseball Stadium. The stadium is scheduled to be demolished and replaced by a new ballpark planned to open in 2032.

Kim In-sik, a legendary former manager who led the last championship win before 2015, threw the ceremonial first pitch. Yang Eui-ji, the catcher who had led the team's success in the 2010s and had since returned to Doosan, received it behind the plate.

Sitting in the stands was Dustin Nippert, the American pitcher who had spent seven seasons throwing to that same catcher. He had left the team years ago against his wishes, and I still remember watching him cry in an interview.

Standing in the opposing dugout was Kim Tae-hyoung, the manager who had led the Bears to seven consecutive Korean Series appearances between 2015 and 2021.

After all those years, they were in the same stadium again, this time to say goodbye.

Watching them, I felt as though I were closing a chapter of my own life, a part of my teenage years that I had genuinely loved and cared about.

I had not expected to feel that way about the Bears again.
