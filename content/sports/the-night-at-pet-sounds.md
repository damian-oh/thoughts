I began supporting Manchester City during the 2013–14 season.

At the time, Premier League matches were available to watch for free in South Korea. I watched Yaya Touré play and found myself thinking that someone was actually playing the kind of football I had only imagined.

He seemed to have everything.

He was tall, strong, fast, and technically gifted. He could pass, dribble, shoot, take free kicks, and score goals. More importantly, he could move through almost every part of the pitch and affect the game wherever he went. He seemed capable of doing nearly everything a footballer could be asked to do.

That season, he scored twenty goals in the Premier League alone as a midfielder.

I became a City supporter almost without making a conscious decision to do so.

Some time later, when I was playing football at school, my coach and teammates experimented with using me as a defensive midfielder. I had considerable speed, strength, and endurance, and the idea was to let me move around the pitch and disrupt the opposition wherever I could.

Perhaps they had imagined something resembling Yaya Touré, only with much less passing ability and technique.

The experiment did not last. We could not find a suitable replacement for me at [[The Other Centre-Back|centre-back]], and I eventually returned to my original position. Looking back, I think that was the better decision.

But I still enjoyed the idea of playing that way.

Over the following years, City changed considerably.

Pep Guardiola arrived in 2016. Touré gradually lost his place in the team, but I enjoyed watching City develop into one of the strongest clubs in football. I liked Guardiola's football, even when his decisions were difficult to understand.

The 2021 Champions League final against Chelsea was particularly painful.

Guardiola started İlkay Gündoğan as the deepest midfielder, leaving both Rodri and Fernandinho on the bench. I remember looking at that midfield and going blank.

Chelsea won 1–0.

I had always considered Guardiola an extraordinary manager. Even before City won the Champions League, I thought only Alex Ferguson belonged above him. But there were moments when his decisions in important matches seemed unnecessarily complicated, and that final was the one I remembered most.

Two years later, City reached the Champions League final again.

On the morning of the match in South Korea, I went to Pet Sounds, a bar in Itaewon, with one of my closest friends. He is a saxophonist, and we have known each other since we were seven.

City were playing Inter Milan.

Most people in the bar were not City supporters. In fact, it seemed that only the City supporters were cheering for City, while almost everyone else wanted Inter to win.

When Rodri scored in the second half, I heard cheers and groans at the same time.

City held on.

When the final whistle blew, I cheered and laughed.

After years of watching the team fall short in Europe, City had finally won the Champions League. They had also completed the treble, having already won the Premier League and FA Cup.

Everyone at Pet Sounds congratulated us.

Then the bar played *Don't Look Back In Anger* by Oasis.

Everyone sang along.

I had loved Oasis since I was young. I did not become a City supporter because of them, although I began loving Oasis and City around the same period of my life. Over time, my affection for the two had become connected.

That morning, hearing the song in a bar full of people who had just watched the same match, I thought about how long I had been waiting.

Ten years.

I had watched Yaya Touré dominate the Premier League. I had watched Guardiola build a remarkable team, win title after title, and repeatedly fall short in Europe. I had watched the final against Chelsea and tried to understand what might have been on Guardiola's mind.

Now they had done it.

I did not have much else to say. I was happy, and I sang with everyone else.

Since then, my relationship with City has become a little more complicated.

The club has faced serious proceedings over breaches of the Premier League's financial regulations. When I first heard about the charges, I remember thinking that this was another episode in a controversy that had followed the club for years.

But as the case developed, I began taking it more seriously.

In September 2026, an independent commission found that City had committed serious breaches of the league's financial rules over several seasons. The club appealed the decision, and the process remains ongoing.

I do not want to dismiss the findings simply because I support the club

If they are upheld, I would understand why people might regard City's achievements over the past decade as having damaged the integrity of the competition. I might agree with them.

I also think I will probably [[Firmness Without Finality|continue supporting City]].

I recognize that my relationship with the club is different from that of someone who grew up in Manchester, attended matches at the stadium, or supported the team through generations of family history. Mine began with a player I happened to see on television in South Korea.

These days, I do not follow football closely.

I do not know whether the outcome of the proceedings will change what that morning at Pet Sounds means to me. Perhaps it will not. Perhaps I will come to understand it differently afterward.

At the time, I was standing beside a friend I had known since childhood, watching a team I had supported for ten years win the Champions League.

When the match ended, I cheered and laughed.

When *Don't Look Back In Anger* came on, I sang.
