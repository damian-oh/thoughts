If I had to name my two favourite bands, I would almost always choose Oasis and Nell.

I have already written about [[What Noel Gallagher Knew|Noel Gallagher]], and how studying music made me increasingly uncertain about what it meant to know how to write a good song.

My reasons for admiring Nell are somewhat different.

For a long time, *Healing Process* was my favourite Nell album. Songs such as *오후와의 대화*, *안녕히 계세요*, *Movie*, and *51분 전* expressed a skeptical attitude toward the world, sometimes approaching despair.

What I liked was that the songs did not seem particularly interested in resolving those feelings into something positive. The doubt remained doubt. Despair was not necessarily followed by reassurance. There was no obligation to conclude that everything would eventually be all right.

But I also admired how carefully that music was made.

I have always considered Kim Jong-wan one of the most capable musicians in Korean popular music, particularly in his ability to combine songwriting, arrangement, and sound production. It is one thing to write lyrics that express a particular attitude toward life. It is another to give those words a musical existence that makes them compelling.

Nell seemed unusually good at both.

The melodies were beautiful. The arrangements could be sophisticated without losing their direction. The sounds themselves seemed to carry something that the lyrics did not need to explain.

Years later, Nell released *C*.

I thought it was their most accomplished album, and perhaps the one that sounded most clearly like Nell to me.

I did not feel that they had simply repeated what they had done on *Healing Process*. The compositions, arrangements, and production had developed, but the result still seemed to possess whatever it was that I had come to love about their music.

Then came *COLORS IN BLACK* in 2019, followed by *Moments in between* in 2021.

I was disappointed.

Some of the lyrics seemed unusually direct and conversational. They described ordinary situations in a way that felt unfamiliar coming from Nell. Other songs seemed to follow a musical vocabulary I had not expected from them.

I think I was troubled by how ordinary some of the choices sounded to me.

There were songs that I could imagine another band writing and performing without losing very much of what made them work. I had rarely felt that way about Nell before.

Of course, these were my impressions as a listener. Kim Jong-wan had no obligation to keep making the kind of music I wanted to hear. Nor did my disappointment establish that the songs were poorly written.

However, there were exceptions.

I liked *A to Z* and *유희*. *유희* was relatively straightforward rock music, while *A to Z* offered sonic details that I enjoyed. Both still seemed to carry a quality I recognized as Nell's, although I would have struggled to define exactly what that quality was. Perhaps I was simply repeating an undefined preference.

I could identify what I disliked about certain arrangements or lyrics. I could describe some of the musical choices that I found effective. But none of those observations seemed sufficient to explain why one song sounded like Nell while another, written and performed by the same musicians, did not.

I still cannot offer a satisfactory definition of what makes Nell sound like Nell.

Then, this year, they released *X*.

I approached the album with anticipation and some nervousness. I had been listening to Nell for long enough to know that a new release would matter to me, but the previous two albums had made me uncertain about what to expect.

I listened, and I thought it was excellent.

The songwriting and production impressed me, but what stayed with me most was *Only One*.

There is a passage in which Kim Jong-wan speaks of memories and dreams.

**In Korean:**

*꿈보다 추억이 많아질 때 말없이 사라져 줄 거야*

*아직은 아냐*

*때로는 악몽이지만 넌 언제나 My only one*

**In English:**

*When I have more memories than dreams, I'll slip away without a word*

*Not yet, though*

*Though at times it's a nightmare, you're always my only one*

I found those words unexpectedly touching, and at the same time, I thought about *Healing Process*.

I thought about how much I had liked a band that could sing about doubt and despair without insisting that those feelings needed to become something else. I thought about the years that followed, the remarkable achievement I heard in *C*, and the disappointment I had felt with some of their later music.

I know very little about what Kim Jong-wan has actually experienced. The thoughts expressed in a song are not necessarily a complete account of the person who wrote it. Nor does a sequence of albums provide a reliable history of someone's private life.

I cannot say that he has overcome the doubts expressed in his earlier work. I cannot even say that *Only One* is intended as a statement about his own future.

What I can say is that I have listened to his music for a long time.

I have heard him express attitudes toward life that I recognized in myself. I have admired the care and technical ability with which he made those attitudes audible. I have sometimes been disappointed by his choices, and at other times surprised by how much I liked what he had made.

After all those years, I heard him sing that there were still dreams ahead.

I do not think hope becomes meaningful only after someone has suffered. Nor do I think that despair must eventually be justified by something positive. That was never what I wanted from *Healing Process*.

Perhaps that is part of why *Only One* affected me. I was not hearing a correction of the earlier songs. I was hearing another possibility, expressed by a musician whose work had meant something to me for many years.

Their despair did not have to be merely a stage on the way to hope. I did not need to imagine that everything had led inevitably to this particular song.

It was enough that the song existed now, after all the others.

I told some friends that I had been prepared to leave a comment on Nell's Instagram if the new album turned out badly. I was glad that I did not have to.

It was a joke, of course. But there was some truth behind it. I had wanted to hear something from Nell again, even though I could not quite define what I was waiting for.

I still do not know what it means for Kim Jong-wan to stand where he does now, after all these years, and sing about having more dreams than memories.

But I know something about what it meant to me to hear him sing those words.

I am glad that he is still making music.

I am glad that I still want to listen.
