In 2019, during my first year at Howon University, I worked with my ensemble members on an arrangement of *She's Leaving Home* by The Beatles. We eventually performed it, and it became the most satisfying performance of my life.

Recently, while preparing for a show, I decided to revisit that arrangement. This time, I needed to recreate the parts that my ensemble members had played. I began rebuilding the arrangement in [Bitwig Studio](https://www.bitwig.com/), recording or sequencing the instruments one by one.

For the bass, I borrowed a hollow-body Liverpool bass from my friend and recorded it myself. Its tone suited the arrangement well. However, the playing was another matter. The bass part was not difficult to play, but my playing was nowhere near that of the bassist who had played with us in 2019.

For the drums, I used [Logic Pro](https://www.apple.com/ca/logic-pro/). I thought its virtual instruments sounded a little better than those available in Bitwig Studio. I spent some time adjusting the sound with EQ, but I was never satisfied with it.
I remembered the drummer who had played the original arrangement. It hardly seemed fair to compare his performance with what I had programmed.

There was also an acoustic piano part. Our ensemble had included an excellent jazz pianist, and working out that part myself turned out to be more difficult than I had expected.

Towards the end of the song, there was a synthesizer part intended for its sonic effect. I used [Serum](https://xferrecords.com/products/serum-2), starting with a sawtooth waveform and applying double-stacked LFOs to the filter to create movement in the sound. The original part had been handled by another ensemble member who made electronic music. I thought his work was much better than mine.

Eventually, I had an arrangement that I could use for the performance.
I was somewhat satisfied with it. There were things I could have done better, but apart from the bass, most of the parts were made with built-in virtual instruments or Serum. I did not think spending much more time adjusting them would make a substantial difference.

More importantly, it was sufficient for the show.

I still preferred the performances of the musicians I had played with in 2019. I could identify many things in my version that I thought were inferior.

Identifying those things was not difficult.

I have spent a considerable amount of time examining ideas by [[Rigour as Paralysis|taking them apart]]. I have begun to notice that doing so is comparatively easy for me.

What I find more difficult is taking an idea of my own and pushing it forward.
With the arrangement, I could hear where my version fell short. None of those observations gave me the tracks I needed.

I still had to choose what to play, record it, listen to the result, make changes, and eventually decide that I had done enough. The result was not the best version of the arrangement I could imagine. Instead, it was a version I had actually made.

Lately, I have been spending more time making music and writing. I have been wondering why.

Part of it is that I want to do something beyond examining what is already there. I want to put forward something of my own, even when I can see its limitations.

I do not want to stop questioning things. Nor do I think making something necessarily justifies the decisions that went into it.

But I want to make those decisions, leave something behind, and accept what follows. Making things is one of the ways I can [[Firmness Without Finality|participate in this life]].

The backing tracks are finished, and I am practising the guitar and vocal parts for the show.
