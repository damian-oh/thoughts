A few days ago, after a show, I was talking with a friend who plays in another band. We began discussing AI in music. He said something about creating things. I do not remember his exact words, but he described creation as a process in which the creator becomes so deeply attached to what they are making that it becomes difficult to distinguish the work from themselves.

I told him, "For me, creating something is about yielding an outcome."

What I meant was that I care most about the result. If different methods can produce something I am satisfied with, I do not want to treat one as inherently more valuable than another.

I wrote a song called *Everything I Know*.

When I was making it, I wanted a female vocalist. I did not know anyone around me whose voice matched what I had in mind, so I used an AI vocal. The result was what I wanted, and that was enough for me.

I enjoy parts of making music. I like playing guitar, working out arrangements, and sometimes experimenting with sounds. But the process can also be frustrating, and occasionally painful. The greatest satisfaction usually comes when I listen to the finished song. I do not feel that I have lost something merely because I did not make every part of it myself.

If I need a particular sound and can choose between making it from scratch and using one that already exists, I would generally choose the latter. I am comfortable relying on [[What I Do Not Need to Know|abstractions]].

After all, I am writing a song, not designing every sound that goes into it.

Of course, the sounds matter. Choosing them, arranging them, and deciding whether they belong together are all part of making the music I want to hear. But I do not see why each sound must originate from my own hands for the resulting song to satisfy me.

I also showed my friend how to create music with [Suno](https://suno.com/discover). He wrote his own prompts and seemed impressed by the results. He also said that he would not use AI in his own creative process.

I do not know exactly why he would not use it, or whether his decision had anything to do with what he had said earlier about creation. Other people joined us, and the conversation moved on.

I have thought about what I said since then.

I can understand why someone might value the experience of making something with their own hands. I sometimes enjoy that experience myself. But when I listen to *Everything I Know*, I do not wish I had produced the vocal myself.

I hear the voice I wanted in the song I wrote.
